1.1 Introduction

The subject matters in our books pertain to Krishna Yajur Veda only. If mantras from other Vedic texts are included, a note has been added. Kindly refer to your Guru for the rendering techniques.

We started with the compilation of books in three languages: Sanskrit, Malayalam, and Tamil (and subsequently expanded to Kannada, Telugu, and English / Latin IAST). We have adhered to standards and conventions in letters and notations common across all books. However, minor differences occur in the representation of letters across these languages.

Our Commitment: We are committed to standardising areas where there are minor gaps and limitations. We continuously incorporate feedback from Veda Learning Groups and respected Gurus with whom we have shared our compiled materials.
Important: Our books are not meant for self-learning. Always refer to your Veda Guru or senior practitioners regarding the precise pronunciation and traditional delivery of Vedic mantras.

1.2 Common Conventions and Standards

To ensure consistency, readability, and ease of multi-language study groups, the following standards are systematically applied:

📄 1. Page Integrity (No Split Mantras)
A Mantra or a Ruk shall not be split across pages so that it remains reader-friendly. Where exceptionally long mantras occur (such as in the Mahanarayana Upanishad) that cannot easily fit onto a single page, appropriate continuity formatting is provided.
🔢 2. Synchronized Multi-Language Numbering
Paragraph numbers and numbering conventions across books are kept identical across all languages. This allows students in a study group using texts in different scripts (Sanskrit, Tamil, Malayalam, Kannada, Telugu) to cross-reference and follow along seamlessly.
⏸ 3. Separator Symbols | and ||
We use the single separator symbol | to indicate the end of a Ruk. The double separator symbol || is used to indicate the end of a mantra rather than a Chandas as per traditional Vedic convention. This convention is chosen purely for reader convenience.
🔤 4. Standard Transliteration & Typography
Standard conversion software is used for the transliteration of source words into Sanskrit, Tamil, Malayalam, Kannada, and Telugu. Specific phonetic font representations and symbols are adapted to the nuances of each language.
🏷 5. Four-Digit Hierarchical Reference System
Wherever possible, references to the Taittiriya Samhita, Brahmanam, and Aranyakam are provided via a four-digit standard notation:
  • 1st digit: Kandam (major section)
  • 2nd digit: Prasna (subject)
  • 3rd digit: Anuvakam (chapter number in that subject)
  • 4th digit: Serial number of the Mantra or Panchaati
📈 6. Running Total Notation
In select subjects, the fourth digit is replaced with the sequential running number of the paragraph to indicate the cumulative total number of Panchaatis in that subject.
🔄 7. Pada Bhedham (Text Variations in Brackets)
Alternate words are provided in brackets whenever there are recognized Pada bhedhams (recitation differences) between reputable traditional sources, or where grammatical rules permit two valid representations of letters.

1.3 Common Symbols Used

The Hyphen (–): Used between words to indicate that the word has parts which need to be rendered together as per your Guru’s teachings.

This sign is used to split long compound words, words constructed through Sandhis resulting in joined letters, and to assist visual parsing and fluent chanting. Usages of hyphens slightly vary in Sanskrit, Malayalam, and Tamil books based on linguistic requirements.

Critical Rule: The hyphen should NOT be taken as a break or pause. Chanting should continue smoothly across hyphenated components as taught by your Guru.

1.4 Special Notes for Tamil Book Readers

Because the Tamil script traditionally possesses a single glyph for each varga consonant, special notations are introduced in VedaVMS Tamil editions to faithfully represent the four Sanskrit consonant variants and subtle Vedic phonetic distinctions:

Tamil Notation Phonetic Explanation & Usage
க2, ச2, ட2, த3, ப4 Consonant Varga Variants (Subscript Numbers):
To represent the four phonetic variants of க, ச, ட, த, ப, a subscript number is appended after the consonant (e.g. க2 = ख, த3 = द, ப4 = भ) to indicate whether it is the 2nd, 3rd, or 4th phonetic variant.
ரு (in italics) Vocalic 'Ru':
Rendered as in Rushi, Amrutam, chanted without lips converging.
ஶ வர்க்கம் Talavya Sibilant:
Pronounced as the "sha" in Shankar (represented as श in Sanskrit Devanagari).
‘ந’ and ‘ன’ Nasal Consonant representation:
In Sanskrit there is only a single dental nasal न. In Tamil editions, both ‘ந’ and ‘ன’ are employed based on Tamil orthographic context, but both represent the exact same Sanskrit sound.
